Collision at an Angle
Oblique collisions, one small step at a time

Every “collision at an angle” question in the ALLEN JEE module (Centre of Mass, sections 4.3–4.4) and its PYQ exercises: Illustrations 37–39, Beginner’s Box 5, Worked Illustrations 6 & 7, Exercise 1–3, and the JEE Main / Advanced PYQs of Exercise 4(A), 4(B) and 5. Every rung shows its source tag.

The one rule behind all of it: break every velocity into an n-part (along the line of impact) and a t-part (along the common tangent). Smooth surfaces never change the t-part. Momentum and e work along n. For free bodies, also keep momentum along x and y separately.

The flow: momentum as a vector → sticking at an angle → pieces flying apart → n and t axes → walls → corners and two walls → inclines → the ground → two discs → the 90° rule → one ball on two → a ball on a string → a free wedge.

Each ladder has 5 or 6 rungs: small build-up steps, each teaching one idea, then the ★ module question. Every ladder is marked DISCUSS IN CLASS or HOMEWORK. 13 ladders, 72 questions. Take g = 10 m/s2 unless the question says otherwise.
